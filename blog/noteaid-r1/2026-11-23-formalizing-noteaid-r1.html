Formalizing NoteAid-R1

Draft · November 23, 2026

What I am trying to solve

How can we train our model on simulated cases?

  • We want to optimize at the scenario level, per session.
  • We do not optimize per turn, although this could improve the model turn by turn as well.

The problem

Setting

  • Cases c \in C: a note, a patient profile and a persona. For each case, N = 8 trajectories \tau_{i,c}, i = 1..N, sampled from \pi_\theta.
  • \sigma_t: the harness's memory (stage, topic, topic slots, resume point, counters, plus private memory). L(\sigma_t): legal moves. h_t: the dialogue so far. r_t: retrieval results.

One turn = two actions of one policy

An action a is one call's whole output; \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s) = \sum_k \log \pi_\theta(a_k \mid s, a_{<k}).

  • Update: s^u_t = \phi_u(c, h_t, \sigma_t, r_t), a^u_t \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s^u_t) with its move in L(\sigma_t), then \sigma'_t = f(\sigma_t, a^u_t).
  • Utterance: s^y_t = \phi_y(c, h_t, \sigma'_t, r_t), a^y_t \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s^y_t).
  • Patient: p_{t+1} \sim P_{sim}(\cdot \mid c, h_t, a^y_t), h_{t+1} = h_t \cup \{a^y_t, p_{t+1}\}.

\phi rebuilds every prompt, so the states are observations, not one growing sequence. The update action both picks a move and writes part of \sigma, so the model partly writes its own memory.

Trajectory: \tau_{i,c} = (s^u_1, a^u_1, s^y_1, a^y_1, s^u_2, …, s^y_T, a^y_T)

What we score

  • V(\tau): session score = coverage (DischargeBench's topic checklist score; Jang et al., 2026) combined with strategy (the six functions of medical communication; King & Hoppe, 2013). How the two combine is still open.
  • \ell(s,a) \ge 0: the action's own fault. Update: skipped the patient's request, set up a repeat, reopened a covered point. Utterance: ignored the request, repeated itself, re-taught a covered point.

Coverage under a responsiveness budget

\max_\theta \; \mathbb{E}_{c \sim C}\, \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid c)} [V(\tau)] \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{(s,a) \in \tau} \ell(s,a)\Big] \le \delta \mathcal{L}(\theta, \lambda) = \mathbb{E}\Big[V(\tau) - \lambda \sum_{(s,a) \in \tau} \ell(s,a)\Big] + \lambda\delta, \qquad \lambda \ge 0 \text{ = the price of one fault in units of } V

How

From the Lagrangian to the loss (\lambda fixed within a round)

  1. Policy gradient: \nabla\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{(s,a) \in \tau} \nabla \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)\,(V(\tau) - \lambda \sum_\tau \ell)\big].
  2. Baseline: subtract b_c = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i V(\tau_{i,c}).
  3. Each call answers for its own fault only (a choice, not exact reward-to-go): A_{i,c}(s,a) = [V(\tau_{i,c}) - b_c] - \lambda\,\ell(s,a) The session's credit is shared by every action; a fault is charged to the action the judge names.
  4. Loss (Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025): no std scaling, a constant Z instead of 1/|a|; PPO-style clipping (Schulman et al., 2017)), maximized: J(\theta) = \frac{1}{|C|}\sum_c \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \frac{1}{Z}\sum_{(s,a) \in \tau_{i,c}} \sum_k \min\big(\rho_k A,\ \mathrm{clip}(\rho_k, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) A\big) - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{ref}) with \rho_k = \pi_\theta(a_k \mid s, a_{<k}) / \pi_{old}(a_k \mid s, a_{<k}). On the first pass over fresh rollouts \rho = 1, so J reduces to REINFORCE (Williams, 1992) with a case baseline plus KL.
    • Why no std scaling: it up-weights cases whose 8 sessions score alike, which is mostly judge noise.
    • Why no 1/|a|: it favors short good outputs and lets long bad ones off lightly (Liu et al., 2025).

Design choices

  • KL reference: the round's starting model (\pi_{ref} = \pi_{old}), GRPO's per-token estimator (Shao et al., 2024); \beta small, possibly 0, decided from the per-round checks.
  • \lambda fixed: one fault costs about the typical spread of V among a case's 8 sessions; the fault rate is reported every round and \lambda is raised by hand if it stays over \delta. Not learned, because 3–6 expensive rounds are too few for it to settle, and a moving \lambda would blur whether a change came from the model or the price. Learned form for later: \lambda \leftarrow \max(0, \lambda + \eta(\text{fault rate} - \delta)).
  • SFT on the best sessions is the special case A = 1 for kept trajectories, 0 otherwise.
  • Combined moves: a patient who is worried but ready gets comfort and progress in one turn (support_and_continue, alongside answer_and_continue), so coverage and responsiveness need not trade off within a turn.

Open questions

  • How coverage and strategy combine into V.
  • \delta per fault kind, and \lambda's value.
  • \beta, \epsilon, Z, passes per round.
