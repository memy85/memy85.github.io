Voice for the virtual patient: a cascaded pipeline

Draft · November 25, 2026

How a spoken turn works

The patient speaks. For now the voice is a cascade of separate parts around a text model (speech to text, the patient model, text to speech), all local open models so no audio leaves the lab. End-to-end speech models may replace it later, once research gives a good starting point.

  • Turn detection. A voice-activity detector hears when speech starts and stops, and a small model decides whether a pause means "I'm done" or "I'm thinking".
  • Speech to text. The educator's words are transcribed while they speak, with the medicine names from the case given as hints.
  • Patient model. The patient's state drives what it says, including hesitation ("um…", "I guess…") when understanding or trust is low.
  • Text to speech. Each persona has a fixed voice; the state sets the emotion and pace. Speaking starts on the first full sentence, to cut the delay.
  • Interruptions. If the educator talks over the patient, playback stops and the rest of the reply is dropped. The patient can also cut in, according to its persona.

Where the models are

  • Turn detection: two small neural networks. A voice-activity detector reads the audio, and a small language model reads the words so far to judge whether the turn is over. No large language model is needed.
  • Speech to text: a neural speech-recognition model. It is a transformer, but not a large language model.
  • Patient model: the only place a large language model is needed. It writes the patient's reply, and for now it also updates the patient's state. Later, a learned world model in the style of JEPA may update the state instead (see the project overview).
  • Text to speech: a neural speech-synthesis model, which takes the voice and emotion settings from the state.
  • Playback and barge-in: no model, only ordinary code that stops the audio when the voice-activity detector hears the educator.
  • Educator: a medical student, or a chatbot such as NoteAid-R1 (a large language model) when both sides are simulated.
